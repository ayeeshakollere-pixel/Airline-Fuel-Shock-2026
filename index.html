When fuel prices doubled, how did four airlines cope?

Delta, United, Ryanair and easyJet, before and after the 2026 Middle East war pushed up the price of jet fuel. Airlines that locked in their fuel price early paid far less extra for fuel, but they still kept less profit from every ticket sold.12

The pattern

Paying less for fuel didn’t mean losing less profit

Ryanair and easyJet paid only about a sixth as much extra for fuel as Delta and United did. Yet every airline lost somewhere between $4.50 and $8 of profit from each $100 of sales.251314

What happened, in plain words

  1. Fuel got much more expensive. The war began in early 2026. The airline industry body expects jet fuel to average about $152 a barrel this year, compared with $90 last year.12
  2. Locking in a price helped a lot with the fuel bill. Ryanair had agreed a price in advance for 80% of its fuel, at about $67 a barrel.8 easyJet had also locked in most of its fuel, about 79% for July to September.11 Their fuel bills rose 16–17% in April to June, against 77–84% for Delta and United.251314
  3. But it didn’t save their profits. All four airlines kept less profit from each $100 of sales in April to June than a year earlier.
  4. The difference was in what they charged. Delta and United earned about 12% more per seat. Ryanair’s average fare fell 6%, and easyJet earned 3% less per seat. Both said the war made customers book later and hesitate.910
  5. The US airlines started to catch up. United won back about half of its extra fuel cost in April to June5 and expected to win back most of it later in the year.16 But fuel is still expensive: Delta expects about $4.25 a gallon this quarter3 and United about $4.80.6
  6. The protection runs out. Ryanair has locked in only 25% of its fuel for the next year, at about $95 a barrel.18

What this page can’t tell you

  • It covers four airlines and one event. It shows what happened, not a rule for every airline.
  • The war hurt more than fuel. It also made some customers hesitate to book, so lower fares are not only a choice.
  • Companies measure profit in slightly different ways, and report in dollars, euros and pounds. That is why the page compares percentages, not money amounts.
  • Numbers marked “est.” are calculated from reported figures. Numbers from news reports should be checked against the company’s own release.
